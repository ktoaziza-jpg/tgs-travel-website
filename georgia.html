Destination Guide

Georgia

Where Majestic Caucasus Peaks Meet the Birthplace of Wine

Nestled between the soaring Greater Caucasus Mountains and the Black Sea coast, Georgia blends 8,000 years of winemaking tradition, UNESCO-listed medieval stone towers, pastel-balconied Tbilisi lanes, and legendary hospitality where every guest is considered a gift from God.

Good to Know

Quick Facts

Currency

Georgian Lari (GEL). Cards are widely accepted in Tbilisi and Batumi; carry cash for mountain villages and local markets.

Language

Georgian (official, with its own unique alphabet). English and Russian are widely spoken in tourist areas.

Time Zone

GET (UTC+4). It is one hour behind Tashkent and Uzbekistan, with no daylight saving time.

Electricity

220V, 50Hz. Uses standard European Type C and F two-pin round plugs.

Must-Visit Places

Top Destinations

Built along the dramatic cliffs of the Mtkvari River, Tbilisi effortlessly fuses ancient Persian-influenced courtyards, Art Nouveau facades, and bold contemporary architecture.

  • Old Town & Narikala Fortress: Ride the cable car to the 4th-century hilltop citadel for panoramic views over terracotta rooftops and carved wooden balconies.
  • Abanotubani Sulfur Baths: Unwind beneath brick-domed bathhouses fed by the natural hot springs that gave Tbilisi its name ("Warm Place").
  • Mtskheta Day Trip: Just 25 minutes away sits Georgia's ancient spiritual capital, home to the UNESCO-listed Jvari Monastery and Svetitskhoveli Cathedral.

Reached via the legendary Georgian Military Highway, this high-alpine valley is framed by jagged glaciers and the 5,054-meter peak of Mount Kazbek.

  • Gergeti Trinity Church: An iconic 14th-century stone church perched at 2,170 meters, standing watch against a dramatic wall of snow-capped summits.
  • Ananuri & Gudauri Viewpoint: Stop along the route at the turquoise Zhinvali Reservoir, medieval Ananuri Fortress, and sweeping canyon panoramas.
  • Truso & Juta Valleys: Hike past mineral springs, abandoned stone fortresses, and alpine meadows beneath the "Georgian Dolomites" of Chaukhi Pass.

Eastern Georgia's sun-drenched Alazani Valley is the birthplace of winemaking, famed for rolling vineyards backed by the snowy Caucasus ridge.

  • Traditional Qvevri Wineries: Taste UNESCO-recognized amber and Saperavi wines fermented underground in giant beeswax-lined clay amphorae.
  • Sighnaghi ("City of Love"): Stroll along cobblestone lanes and 18th-century defensive ramparts overlooking the vast valley below.
  • Tsinandali & Alaverdi: Explore aristocratic 19th-century wine estates, lush gardens, and centuries-old monastic cellars.

Western Georgia delivers extraordinary contrasts — from Europe's highest permanently inhabited medieval villages to palm-lined coastal promenades.

  • Mestia & Ushguli (Svaneti): Discover UNESCO-protected stone Svan watchtowers dating back to the 9th century beneath Mount Shkhara's glaciers.
  • Batumi: Relax along the subtropical Black Sea coast, stroll the famous seaside boulevard, and visit the lush Batumi Botanical Garden.
Visa & Entry

One of the World's Most Welcoming Borders

Georgia offers one of the most generous entry policies on earth. Citizens of over 90 countries — including Uzbekistan, the EU, UK, USA, Canada, Australia, UAE, Japan, and CIS nations — can enter visa-free for up to one full year (365 days).

Travelers from non-exempt nationalities can apply easily through Georgia's official electronic visa (e-Visa) portal, which typically processes applications online within 5 working days without an embassy visit.

Please ensure you carry valid travel health insurance for the duration of your stay. Share your passport details with our team, and we will verify entry requirements and assist with every step before departure.

Climate Guide

When to Go

From blooming spring cities and summer alpine passes to autumn grape harvests and winter ski slopes, Georgia is a true four-season destination.

Blooming Valleys & Mild Sightseeing 🌸

Spring awakens Georgia with cherry and almond blossoms in Tbilisi, lush green hills in Kakheti, and comfortably warm weather for walking tours before peak summer arrives.

Avg. Temp15–23°C
High PassesMelting by late May
Best ForTbilisi, Mtskheta, Kakheti

Alpine Treks & Black Sea Sun ☀️

Summer opens Georgia's highest mountain roads and trekking trails in Kazbegi, Tusheti, and Svaneti, while Batumi comes alive with warm sea breezes and coastal festivals.

Avg. Temp25–34°C
Mountain NightsCool (10–16°C)
Best ForKazbegi, Svaneti, Batumi

Rtveli Wine Harvest & Golden Foliage 🍂

Widely considered the finest season in Georgia. September and October bring crisp sunny days, blazing golden mountain forests, and Rtveli — the joyous annual grape harvest festival in Kakheti.

Avg. Temp14–24°C
HighlightRtveli Grape Harvest
Best ForKakheti, Tbilisi, Kazbegi

Caucasus Ski Resorts & Cozy Supras ❄️

Winter turns the Greater Caucasus into a premier powder destination. Ski world-class freeride slopes in Gudauri or Mestia, then warm up in Tbilisi's steaming sulfur baths and fireplace wine cellars.

Avg. Temp-2–8°C
Snow LayerDeep powder in mountains
Best ForGudauri, Bakuriani, Tbilisi
Logistics

Getting Around Georgia

Flights & Arrival

Direct flights connect Tashkent, Europe, and the Middle East with Tbilisi International Airport (TBS), as well as Batumi (BUS) and Kutaisi (KUT). We arrange private meet-and-greet transfers straight to your hotel.

Private Mountain Vehicles

Georgia's compact geography makes scenic road trips effortless. We provide comfortable sedans, minivans, and rugged 4x4 vehicles with veteran local drivers for winding passes like Kazbegi and Svaneti.

Rail & Boutique Estates

Modern double-decker Stadler trains glide between Tbilisi and Batumi in just 5 hours. Across the country, we curate charming design hotels, panoramic mountain lodges, and family-run Kakheti wine chateaux.

Destination Guide

Armenia

The Land of Ancient Monasteries, Pink Stone & Mount Ararat

An open-air museum cradled by volcanic highlands and deep river canyons, Armenia is home to one of the world's oldest civilizations. From the warm pink tufa boulevards of Yerevan to cliffside monasteries overlooking Mount Ararat, every corner resonates with living history, music, and heartfelt warmth.

Good to Know

Quick Facts

Currency

Armenian Dram (AMD). Cards work smoothly across Yerevan; carry cash for rural monasteries, markets, and guesthouses.

Language

Armenian (official). Russian is widely understood everywhere, and English is commonly spoken in Yerevan and by our guides.

Time Zone

AMT (UTC+4). It is one hour behind Tashkent and Uzbekistan, with no daylight saving time.

Electricity

220V, 50Hz. Uses standard European Type C and F two-pin round plugs.

Must-Visit Places

Top Destinations

Older than Rome by 29 years, Yerevan glows in warm rose-tinted volcanic tufa stone and pulses with jazz clubs, wine streets, and open-air terraces.

  • Cascade Complex & Republic Square: Climb monumental limestone stairways lined with contemporary sculptures for sweeping views of Mount Ararat, then catch the evening singing fountains.
  • Matenadaran: Explore one of the world's richest repositories of medieval illuminated manuscripts and ancient scientific texts.
  • Vernissage & Ararat Brandy Factory: Browse handcrafted carpets, woodcarvings, and silver jewelry before tasting legendary aged Armenian brandy.

Within an hour of Yerevan lie Armenia's most storied architectural wonders, framed by dramatic basalt canyons and snow-covered summits.

  • Khor Virap Monastery: Perched above the Ararat Plain, offering the closest and most iconic panoramic backdrop of twin-peaked Mount Ararat.
  • Garni Temple: A stunning 1st-century Greco-Roman colonnaded temple standing above the hexagonal basalt cliffs of the "Symphony of Stones."
  • Geghard Monastery: A UNESCO World Heritage sanctuary partially carved straight out of solid mountain rock, famous for its haunting acoustics.

Journey north into crisp highland air where turquoise alpine water meets misty woodland valleys often nicknamed "Armenian Switzerland."

  • Lake Sevan & Sevanavank: Climb the peninsula steps to 9th-century black-tufa chapels gazing across one of the world's largest high-altitude freshwater lakes (1,900m).
  • Dilijan National Park: Hike through dense oak and beech forests to serene Parz Lake and the hidden medieval complex of Haghartsin Monastery.
  • Historic Old Dilijan: Wander timber-balconied Sharambeyan Street, home to traditional ceramicists, woodworkers, and cozy mountain bistros.

Southern Armenia rewards travelers with terracotta rock canyons, archaeological breakthroughs, and soaring aerial vistas.

  • Wings of Tatev: Ride the world's longest reversible aerial tramway (5.7 km) across the deep Vorotan Gorge to the 9th-century cliffside fortress-monastery of Tatev.
  • Noravank Monastery: A 13th-century architectural masterpiece glowing orange against towering brick-red limestone cliffs.
  • Areni-1 Wine Cave: Visit the site of the world's oldest known 6,100-year-old winery and sample indigenous Areni Noir vintages in local family cellars.
Visa & Entry

Effortless Entry to the Highlands

Armenia maintains a straightforward and welcoming visa regime. Citizens of Uzbekistan, the EU, UK, USA, CIS states, UAE, Japan, South Korea, and dozens of other countries can enter visa-free for up to 180 days within a year.

For nationalities requiring a visa, Armenia's official electronic visa (e-Visa) system allows travelers to submit their application entirely online, with typical approval within 3 business days — and select nationalities also qualify for visa on arrival at Zvartnots Airport.

Send a copy of your passport to our specialists, and we will confirm your exact visa requirements, handle electronic paperwork if needed, and coordinate smooth airport arrival.

Climate Guide

When to Go

Enjoying over 300 sunny days a year, Armenia transforms vividly with each season — from apricot blossoms in spring to golden gorges in autumn.

Apricot Blossoms & Emerald Gorges 🌸

Spring dresses the Ararat Valley in fragrant apricot blossoms, wild poppies carpet the canyons, and crisp, sunny mornings deliver crystal-clear views of snow-draped Mount Ararat.

Avg. Temp14–22°C
Mountain TrailsFresh & blooming
Best ForYerevan, Garni, Khor Virap

High-Altitude Lakes & Warm Nights ☀️

While Yerevan basks in dry sunshine and lively late-night café culture, the high mountain passes, shaded Dilijan forests, and cool breezes of Lake Sevan offer a refreshing summer escape.

Avg. Temp26–36°C
Lake SevanRefreshing (18–24°C)
Best ForLake Sevan, Dilijan, Tatev

Velvet Season & Areni Wine Festival 🍂

Golden autumn is Armenia at its most magical. Mild sunshine illuminates crimson forests in Dilijan, markets overflow with pomegranates and peaches, and October brings the celebrated Areni Wine Festival.

Avg. Temp15–25°C
HighlightAreni Wine Festival (Oct)
Best ForDilijan, Noravank, Vineyards

Snowy Monasteries & Ski Slopes ❄️

Snow blankets medieval stone sanctuaries in peaceful silence, Tsaghkadzor opens its sunlit ski slopes, and Yerevan glows with festive lights, cozy wine bars, and steaming bowls of hearty winter cuisine.

Avg. Temp-5–5°C
Snow LayerHeavy in highlands

Getting Around Armenia

Flights & Arrival

International travelers arrive at Zvartnots International Airport (EVN), located just 20 minutes from central Yerevan. Our team greets you on arrival and coordinates private transfers directly to your hotel.

Scenic Private Drives

Most iconic sights can be reached via comfortable day trips or multi-day loops from Yerevan. We provide modern air-conditioned vehicles and SUVs with experienced local drivers familiar with every mountain pass.

Cableways & Boutique Stays

Soar across the Vorotan Gorge aboard the Wings of Tatev aerial tramway, and unwind in handpicked boutique hotels in central Yerevan, lakeside retreats on Sevan, and forest lodges in Dilijan.

Before You Pack

Traveler Tips & FAQ

Armenian cuisine celebrates sun-ripened produce, wild mountain herbs, and wood-fired traditions. Must-try dishes include freshly baked Lavash flatbread (UNESCO intangible heritage), charcoal-grilled Khorovats, grape-leaf Tolma, grilled Lake Sevan trout, fragrant pomegranate wine, and aged Armenian brandy. Abundant salads and herb-filled Zhingyalov Hats flatbreads make dining easy for vegetarians as well.
Early morning just after sunrise offers the clearest, crispest views of Mount Ararat — especially from Khor Virap Monastery or the top of Yerevan's Cascade — before warm afternoon haze or mountain clouds gather around the peak.
Armenia is famous for its cold, pure spring water — you will spot public stone drinking fountains (pulpulaks) all around Yerevan and mountain towns. Local mobile providers (Viva-MTS, Team Telecom, Ucom) offer affordable SIMs and eSIMs with reliable 4G across cities and main tourist routes.
